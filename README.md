# Hi, I'm Sheraz

I'm a student in Lahore, Pakistan, and most of what I build starts with a question I ran into close to home: how well AI explains things in Urdu, how electricity gets shared out on Pakistan's grid, and how much the school a child attends decides what they learn.

## What I'm building

**[Wattlas](https://wattlas.org)** is a browser-based model of Pakistan's national electricity grid, built from NTDC planning documents. It's endorsed by NEPRA, the national power regulator, and has been used by more than 46,000 people. Its companion tool, Bijli Baba, helps households work out how to lower their electricity bills, and it brings in most of the traffic.

**[Rehnuma](https://www.rehnuma.study)** is an Urdu-English AI tutor now running in 11 government schools in Punjab, in partnership with Noon Academy. It explains lessons through analogies drawn from village life in Punjab.

**[Roshni](https://github.com/sherazhassan7/roshni)** explains BISP and government benefit messages in plain Urdu, reads them aloud, and flags the ones that look like scams.

**[TechSeed](https://www.techseed.tech)** is a nonprofit I founded to teach AI literacy in Urdu, in person and online, in partnership with Allah Walay Trust. Students learn to use AI, build with it, and question it.

## Research

**[UrduExplainBench](https://github.com/sherazhassan7/UrduExplainBench)** compares how well language models explain Grade 6 to 10 science and maths in Urdu versus English. I built it as a student researcher at the Center for Language Engineering, UET Lahore. Urdu explanations scored about a point lower on a 5-point scale, and the paper is under review.

**[Educational inequality in Pakistan](https://github.com/sherazhassan7/educational-inequality-pakistan)** is a district-level analysis of the learning gap between government and private schools, using ASER 2023 data on about 214,000 children. I wrote about it for *Pakistan Today* in ["We Never Did the Sums"](https://www.pakistantoday.com.pk/2026/07/28/we-never-did-the-sums-2).
